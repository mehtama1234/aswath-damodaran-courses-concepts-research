Overview Thesis Themes Concepts Evidence Sessions

Concept

Market-level pricing

Meaning. Pricing logic also applies to whole markets, where rates, macro narratives, tariffs, and mood shape the multiples investors will pay.

Why it matters. It widens the course from company-specific analysis into broader consumer, policy, and market context.

How the course develops it. The tariff and market-pricing discussion shows how valuation ideas interact with public narratives and macro repricing.

Worked example

In January 2022, the S&P 500 traded at 18.5x trailing P/E. Implied in that multiple are assumptions about growth, risk, and rates. If the 10-year Treasury rises 150 bps (from 1.5% to 3%), the risk-free rate for all discounting rises 150 bps. Cost of equity for an average S&P firm rises from 8% to 9.5%. If earnings and growth stay the same, the justified P/E compresses: E / (r - g) = E / (0.095 - 0.03) = E / 0.065 instead of E / 0.05. The multiple falls from 20x to 15.4x. A $100B market cap justified at 18.5x ($1.85T market cap) drops to $1.54T at 15.4x—$310B wiped out before any company fundamentals change. That's how macro repricing works. Tariffs add to the calculation: if Trump's tariffs are expected to lower S&P 500 earnings 5% (~$60 per share), and the S&P earnings are ~$210/share, adjusted earnings = $200/share. The market multiple compresses further because earnings shrank.

Conversely, if rates fall or growth expectations rise, multiples expand. Mid-2023, when Fed rate cuts became expected, S&P 500 P/E moved from 15x to 19x, lifting valuations 27% with no earnings change.

Where the approach breaks

Market-level pricing fails when analysts treat valuation mechanics (rates, multiples, risk premiums) as disconnected from macro reality. The 2021 tech bubble showed peak overconfidence: SaaS multiples stayed at 15-20x revenue for firms with 30-40% churn despite rising rates (which should have compressed multiples). Investors convinced themselves "growth changes the discount rate" independently of what the Treasury market was pricing. When rates spiked December 2021 and January 2022, the multiple collapse ($2T+ wiped from tech in 12 weeks) surprised those who believed the story mattered more than the rate. The failure also occurs when macro narratives ("AI will change everything" or "housing is safe") override math. 2006-2007, housing multiples stayed high because the narrative was "housing always appreciates," but the fundamentals (rates, affordability, household income growth) said prices had decoupled. Tariff impacts show a similar failure: companies assume tariffs are temporary and don't adjust discount rates or growth assumptions, leaving valuations disconnected from the actual earnings headwind. Market-level pricing discipline means: Check the discount rate against risk-free rates. Check growth against GDP growth. If your justified multiple doesn't match current prices, ask why the market might know something you don't rather than assuming the market is wrong.
