Concept

Distressed equity as option

Meaning. In distress, equity can behave like a contingent claim on recovery rather than a normal residual ownership interest.

Why it matters. It shows how capital structure and downside asymmetry can preserve equity value even when conventional valuation looks bleak.

How the course develops it. The distressed-equity session broadens option logic beyond technology and natural-resource cases.

Worked example

A retailer in distress has enterprise value $500M (estimated by FCFF DCF). Debt outstanding: $400M. Residual equity value = $500M - $400M = $100M using standard intrinsic-value math. But equity is not a normal residual; it is a call option on the enterprise value. Under a recovery scenario (new management, cost restructuring), EV rises to $700M; equity = $300M. Under liquidation, EV falls to $300M; equity = $0 (debt gets priority). Equity holders win if recovery happens ($300M gain on their claim) and lose if liquidation happens (total loss). The volatility of recovery outcomes is high—equity behaves like a deep out-of-the-money call, not like a normal equity residual. A call option model might value equity at $120M, not $100M, because of the asymmetry: limited downside (already near zero) but upside of $300M if restructuring succeeds.

Probability-weighted: 60% recovery (equity = $300M), 40% liquidation (equity = $0) → Expected equity = $180M. This exceeds the simple $100M residual because volatility and asymmetry create option value.

Where the approach breaks

Distressed-equity-as-option breaks when the option is not really an option—when debt recovery is so certain that equity has no upside. If debt is $400M and enterprise value is nearly certain to be $450M, equity's payoff is capped: equity = $50M, not much optionality. The frame also breaks if debt default is imminent and unrecoverable; then equity is truly worthless and no amount of volatility helps. Red flag: using Black-Scholes on distressed equity when the underlying asset (the company) has visibility only 2-3 years out, not 30 years. Distress equity options are shorter-dated and binary (recovery or failure), not smooth diffusion processes. Historical case: Lehman Brothers equity in 2008 looked worthless via option models because bankruptcy risk dominated, but some investors guessed wrong and bought at $0.21, hoping bankruptcy would be delayed or asset recoveries would surprise. The boundary is when macro defaults and fire sales trump operating recovery.
