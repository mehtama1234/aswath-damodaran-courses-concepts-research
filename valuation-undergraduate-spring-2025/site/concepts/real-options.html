Concept

Real options

Meaning. Real options treat uncertainty as a potential source of value when a firm controls a scarce right to wait, expand, or change course.

Why it matters. It is the course's clearest break from the idea that uncertainty only belongs in a higher discount rate.

How the course develops it. The IPO-to-options transition and the patents/resources block show when optionality is real and when it is being abused rhetorically.

Worked example

A pharmaceutical company holds a patent on an experimental cancer drug. Expected clinical trial cost is $150M over 5 years. If trials succeed (30% probability), the drug's DCF value is $3B; if they fail (70%), it's worth $0. Naive NPV = 0.30 × $3B - $150M = $750M. But the firm can wait 2 years for early-stage data before committing the full $150M investment. This 'wait option' is valuable: in 2 years, trial success signals improve (say, to 50% vs. 30% today). If early data flops, the firm abandons before spending $150M. The value of waiting ≈ (avoid 70% × $150M bad-outcome cost) + (higher success probability signal upside). In Black-Scholes terms, this wait option is worth $20–40M. Ignoring it and committing $150M today leaves $20–40M in option value on the table.

Where the approach breaks

Real options fail as a framework when two conditions hold: (1) No actual decision flexibility exists. A mining company with a 5-year ore extraction concession cannot 'wait and see'—ore degrades, equipment depreciates, and delay destroys value. Calling this a real option is rhetoric. (2) Optionality is already embedded in cash-flow forecasts. If an analyst projects $3B in drug value by assuming a 30% success probability and then adds option value on top, she double-counts uncertainty. The option value only adds if the DCF assumed certainty and you are adjusting for the ability to wait and learn. A third failure: overusing options to justify speculative investments. Every startup has a 'platform option' or 'network effects option,' but if burn rates and competitive dynamics mean the firm won't survive long enough to exercise the option, the option is worthless. Real options require exclusivity, staying power, and a realistic decision point to reset assumptions.
