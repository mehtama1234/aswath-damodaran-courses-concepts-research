Pricing Is A Different Language From Intrinsic Value

Intrinsic value versus pricing

Intrinsic value asks what the asset is worth from its own cash flows, while pricing asks what similar assets trade for in a market.

Pricing Is A Different Language From Intrinsic Value

Pricing multiples

Multiples compress assumptions about growth, risk, and profitability into a market ratio that looks simple only because the assumptions are hidden.

Pricing Is A Different Language From Intrinsic Value

Peer groups and comparables

Relative pricing depends on which firms are judged similar enough to act as comparables and how those similarities are defined.

Pricing Is A Different Language From Intrinsic Value

Market-level pricing

Pricing logic also applies to whole markets, where rates, macro narratives, tariffs, and mood shape the multiples investors will pay.

Private-company valuation

Private-company valuation changes when the owners are undiversified, liquidity is limited, and control rights are structured differently from public markets.

Asset-based valuation and pricing

Uncertainty Can Create Optionality, Not Just A Higher Discount Rate

Real options

Real options treat uncertainty as a potential source of value when a firm controls a scarce right to wait, expand, or change course.

Patents and resource options
