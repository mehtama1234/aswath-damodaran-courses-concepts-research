DCF big picture

A DCF is the course's organizing architecture for intrinsic value: expected cash flows discounted at rates that match risk, currency, and claim definition.

riskfree-ratescash-flow-constructionterminal-value-discipline

Why it matters: It turns valuation from a loose idea into an operating framework that can be built and checked piece by piece.

Sessions 4, 5, 8, 17, 6

Riskfree rates

The riskfree rate is chosen to match the currency and inflation setting of the valuation, not copied blindly from a default reference bond.

equity-risk-premiumsbetas-and-relative-riskcost-of-capital

Why it matters: It anchors every later discount-rate choice, so a bad starting rate contaminates the whole model.

Sessions 5, 6, 4, 7, 8

Equity risk premiums

The equity risk premium is the market's current price of bearing equity risk over the riskfree alternative.

riskfree-ratesbetas-and-relative-riskcost-of-capital

Why it matters: It is one of the largest levers in any valuation and a direct bridge from market pricing into intrinsic-value inputs.

Beta is used as a way to connect company risk back to market risk, but only after thinking through business mix and leverage.

Intrinsic value versus pricing

Intrinsic value asks what the asset is worth from its own cash flows, while pricing asks what similar assets trade for in a market.

pricing-multiplespeer-groups-and-comparablesasset-based-valuation

Why it matters: This separation is one of the course's most durable intellectual distinctions and a major protection against analytical confusion.

Sessions 19, 18, 21, 22, 11

Pricing multiples

Multiples compress assumptions about growth, risk, and profitability into a market ratio that looks simple only because the assumptions are hidden.

Why it matters: It helps students see why comparables are powerful in markets but still require disciplined interpretation.

Relative pricing depends on which firms are judged similar enough to act as comparables and how those similarities are defined.
