Long-form synthesis

The real thesis of Corporate Finance Spring 2025

Damodaran's course is not mainly a tour of corporate finance formulas. It is a sustained argument about how managers should make value-relevant decisions when risk, agency problems, taxes, side effects, financing constraints, and reinvestment limits all collide.

The course starts with the objective function because the rest of corporate finance has no meaning without a target. It then builds the machinery required to act on that target: hurdle rates, project cash flows, capital structure, debt design, payout policy, and valuation.

Argument spine

  1. Define the end game before choosing tools.
  2. Convert risk into hurdle rates matched to currency, business risk, and financing mix.
  3. Judge investments by incremental cash flows and risk-adjusted returns.
  4. Use debt only when its benefits exceed distress, agency, and flexibility costs.
  5. Design debt to fit operating cash flows.
  6. Return cash when managers cannot reinvest it well.
  7. Use valuation as the final integration of every corporate finance choice.
4 sessions

The Objective Function

Corporate finance starts by asking what a business should maximize, who gets to decide, and what happens when the objective is bent toward other stakeholder claims.

5 sessions

Risk and Hurdle Rates

The cost of capital is built from riskfree rates, equity risk premiums, betas, default spreads, and financing costs rather than taken as a spreadsheet input.

6 sessions

Investment Decision

Good projects are judged on incremental cash flows, time-weighted returns, side costs, side benefits, real options, and post-mortems rather than accounting earnings alone.

7 sessions

Financing Mix

Debt is useful only up to the point where tax benefits, default risk, agency costs, flexibility, and business risk stop improving firm value.

3 sessions

Dividends and Cash Return

Cash return policy is a discipline problem: compare cash generated, reinvestment needs, trust in management, taxes, buybacks, and dividends.

2 sessions

Valuation as the Final Integration

The course closes by tying objective, risk, investment quality, capital structure, and payout into valuation.
