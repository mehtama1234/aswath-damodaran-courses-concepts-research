Hurdle rates

Overview Thesis Themes Subthemes Concepts Evidence Discussions Sessions

Concept

Hurdle rates

Meaning. The minimum required return for an investment, matched to its risk, currency, and financing mix.

Why it matters. Hurdle rates decide what the firm says yes or no to; they translate risk into capital allocation discipline.

How Damodaran develops it. The course treats hurdle rates as built objects: riskfree rate, risk premium, beta, debt cost, and capital mix must all be internally consistent.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same hurdle rate for every project.
  • Confusing accounting return targets with risk-adjusted required returns.

Worked example

A real estate developer evaluates a residential project: $50M upfront, $8M annual operating cash flow for 10 years, $2M terminal value (year 11). CFO sets hurdle at 12% (firm WACC). But project is lower risk: 40% debt at 5%, 60% equity, lower risk than industrial ops (70% debt at 8%, generating 18% IRR). Project-specific WACC: 40% × 5% × (1 - 25%) + 60% × 10% = 7.5%, not 12%. At 7.5%, NPV = sum[8M/1.075^t] + 2M/1.075^11 - 50M = $56.2M - $50M = $6.2M. At 12%, NPV = $45.3M - $50M = -$4.7M.

Using wrong hurdle rejects a value-adding project.

Where the approach breaks

Project hurdles fail when divorced from risk profile. Energy: exploration (30% risk) needs 25%+ hurdle; pipeline (5% risk) needs 8-9%. Applying single 12% hurdle underinvests in stable assets, chases high-risk ventures. Enron used 15% blended; high-margin trading cleared it, merchant power (stable, 8% risk) looked bad, directing capital into risky ventures. Second: confusing hurdle with aspiration. Target 30% debt, 70% equity = 8% WACC; if actual is 50% debt = 10% WACC, but CFO uses 8%, deals hidden by "growth-at-any-cost" are subsidized. Third: sticky hurdles in macro shifts. 10% hurdle set when Treasuries were 2% (8% ERP) becomes obsolete at 4% rates, 5.5% ERP = 9.5% true WACC. Many firms didn't adjust in 2022-2023, killing good projects.
