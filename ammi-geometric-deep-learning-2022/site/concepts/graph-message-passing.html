Concept Page

Graph message passing

Graph neural learning treats nodes, edges, and neighborhoods as the basic geometry of computation.

graphsmessage-passinglocality

Core Idea

Representation learning on graphs is built from local information exchange, with expressivity determined by how messages are formed, aggregated, and updated.

Why It Matters

Graphs are the course's most flexible structured domain and the staging ground for many later applications.

Locality

Information is exchanged through neighborhoods rather than through one flat vector.

Expressivity

The architecture is judged by which graph distinctions it can and cannot represent.

Linked Subthemes
  • Graphs require relation-aware locality and message passing
  • Scientific and expressive graph variants push beyond vanilla message passing
Broader Patterns
  • Graph structure is both a source of power and a bottleneck for expressivity.
  • Many later graph variants are attempts to fix what plain message passing misses.
Applied Uses
  • Molecules, social networks, physical interaction systems, and knowledge graphs.
  • Problems where relations matter at least as much as attributes.
