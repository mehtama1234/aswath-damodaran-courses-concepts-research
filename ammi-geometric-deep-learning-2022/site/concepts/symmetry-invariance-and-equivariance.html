Concept Page

Symmetry, invariance, and equivariance

Symmetry is the course's preferred language for telling a model what should stay unchanged and what should transform predictably.

symmetryinvarianceequivariance

Core Idea

Invariance asks for unchanged outputs under a transformation, while equivariance asks for outputs that transform in a controlled corresponding way.

Why It Matters

It is the conceptual core behind CNNs, group-equivariant models, and much of the later geometry block.

Invariance

The answer should not change when the input is transformed.

Equivariance

The answer should change in a rule-bound way that mirrors the input transformation.

Linked Subthemes
  • Invariance and equivariance formalize what should stay fixed or transform predictably
  • Equivariance and protein structure show the payoff of getting geometry right
Broader Patterns
  • The right symmetry can dramatically reduce sample complexity.
  • Abstract equivariance often becomes valuable only when it is tied back to a domain task.
Applied Uses
  • Image recognition under translation or rotation.
  • Scientific prediction where physical laws are coordinate-independent.
