Concepts

Each concept page compares course-specific variants and links back into the underlying course sites.

Risk

Risk is one of the clearest recurring Damodaran concepts across courses. It appears as a first-principles condition for return, a lived investor tradeoff, a formal corporate finance input, and a valuation building block, with the undergraduate valuation course adding a more visibly scaffolded route into discount-rate logic.

Valuation

Valuation is the integrating language across the active course set. It is where earlier assumptions about business reality, claims, time, and risk must become internally consistent, and it now appears in both undergraduate and MBA valuation forms inside the workspace.

Discipline

A large share of Damodaran's teaching style is about forcing disciplined thinking against seductive shortcuts, loose labels, and inconsistent assumptions, and the undergraduate valuation course adds a clearer execution-focused version of that discipline.

Control

Control appears across both courses as a problem of agency, intervention, and overconfidence.

Trust and Institutions

Both courses repeatedly show that finance depends on trust, legitimacy, authority, and institutional design.

Fit and Constraints

Damodaran repeatedly returns to the fact that frameworks must fit the real actor using them, the real asset being analyzed, and the real nominal environment in which the claim exists. The undergraduate valuation course adds a clearer audience-level version of this idea by showing students where the default public-company template stops fitting the problem.

Capital Allocation

Both courses are deeply concerned with where scarce capital should go, what standards should govern that choice, and how bad allocation decisions compound over time.

Optionality

Damodaran repeatedly returns to the value of future flexibility, but he treats optionality differently depending on whether the decision maker is an investor looking at growth or a manager choosing projects.

Narrative Versus Numbers

A recurring Damodaran tension is that stories matter, but they only become useful when disciplined by numbers, incentives, explicit assumptions, and the actual structure of the claim being discussed. The undergraduate valuation course adds a particularly visible teaching bridge between narrative and model inputs.

Pricing

Damodaran treats pricing as a distinct language from intrinsic valuation, and the workspace now has enough cross-course material to compare how that distinction behaves in investor, valuation, and introductory settings.

Growth and Reinvestment

Across courses, growth matters only when it can be financed, sustained, and converted into value rather than narrative excitement.
