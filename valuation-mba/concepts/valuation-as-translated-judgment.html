Concept

Valuation as translated judgment

Meaning. Valuation is a disciplined translation of a business story into explicit assumptions about growth, margins, reinvestment, risk, and time.

Why it matters. This is the course's deepest habit: a model is useful only when its numbers expose the story rather than hiding it.

How Damodaran develops it. The opening and closing sessions keep returning to valuation as a craft of judgment under uncertainty, not spreadsheet completion.

Common mistakes

  • Building a model before knowing the story.
  • Treating precision in cells as proof of analytical precision.

Worked example

Take a SaaS company with $10M revenue growing 25% annually. The analyst translates that into: market size 2B, capture 1%, maturity in 8 years, then 3% perpetual growth. Margins: 5% now, 30% terminal (improving as scale hits fixed costs). Reinvestment: $2M capex plus $1M working capital increase in year 1, declining to $0.5M by year 3 as efficiency gains. Cost of equity 12%, tax 21%. The DCF is worth $95M only if all those assumptions hold together. Change margin trajectory or growth capture and the value swings to $60M or $150M. The model is useless if it just outputs a number; it is useful only when reading it back reveals whether the story is credible. Can the company really achieve 30% margins in a competitive market? If not, the model catches the lie.

Where the approach breaks

This breaks when the analyst builds the model first and reads the story from it afterward. A common failure: analyst assumes 20% growth for 10 years without checking what market share that implies. The model says the company will be larger than the entire addressable market—an impossible story. Real case: early-stage AI startups in 2022 where analysts projected $1B revenue by year 5 but never asked how the startup would acquire 30% of enterprise software spend in that timeframe. The discipline fails when the modeler is emotionally committed to the valuation answer and reverses the causality—fitting stories to match the spreadsheet output instead of building spreadsheets from honest stories.
