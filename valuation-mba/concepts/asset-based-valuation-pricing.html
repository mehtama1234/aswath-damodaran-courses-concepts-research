Concept

Asset-based valuation and pricing

Meaning. Asset-based approaches anchor value or price in what the underlying assets are worth, especially when going-concern cash-flow logic is weak.

Why it matters. They matter in liquidation, holding-company, commodity, financial, and distressed settings where operating forecasts are less reliable.

How Damodaran develops it. The pricing closure introduces asset-based valuation and pricing as a different anchor after multiples.

Common mistakes

  • Using book value as market value without adjustment.
  • Applying asset-based logic to businesses whose value is mainly intangible growth.

Worked example

A regional bank holds $10B in loans (book value), earns 4% net interest margin on them, but markets are pricing similar loans at 2.5% yield (implying a 37.5% markdown on asset value to reflect credit risk and market yield). Replacement cost approach: if you liquidate today, you realize $10B × (2.5% / 4%) ≈ $6.25B. Asset-based value estimate = $6.25B. The bank also has $2B in deposits and $1.5B tangible equity (book). Adjust equity value: $6.25B - $2B = $4.25B, not the $10B book equity shown. This tells management: even if operations are stable, the market reprices core assets down 37.5% based on current yield curves and credit conditions.

Where the approach breaks

Asset-based approaches fail when intangible value dominates. Apple's balance sheet shows ~$30B in tangible assets but trades at >$3T because iOS, brand, supply chain, and ecosystem are worth >$2.9T and don't appear on the asset side. Using asset-based logic here produces a near-worthless estimate. The second failure is cyclical repricing: in mark-to-market downturns, asset values can collapse faster than operating fundamentals warrant. March 2020: mortgage-backed securities plunged 40% in days even though default rates hadn't risen that fast. A bank relying on asset-based value then got ambushed. Asset-based works best for companies trading below liquidation value (rare) or for mature, low-growth firms (utilities, REITs) where cash flows are predictable and replacement cost is stable.
