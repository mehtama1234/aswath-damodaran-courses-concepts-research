Reinvestment

Overview Thesis Themes Subthemes Concepts Evidence Discussions Sessions

Concept

Reinvestment

Meaning. Reinvestment is the capital required to generate and sustain growth, including capex, working capital, acquisitions, and other growth investments.

Why it matters. It is the cost side of the growth story. Growth without reinvestment discipline is usually fantasy.

How Damodaran develops it. Damodaran ties growth to sales-to-capital ratios, return on capital, and reinvestment needs through the middle sessions.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming growth is free.
  • Using high terminal growth without matching reinvestment.

Worked example

A hardware manufacturer earns operating income of $100M on $500M revenue (20% margin). To grow revenue by 8% annually, it must invest $50M in capex annually and increase working capital by $10M. Reinvestment rate = ($50M + $10M) / $100M = 60%. If return on invested capital (ROIC) is 12%, sustainable growth = ROIC × Reinvestment rate = 12% × 60% = 7.2%. This matches the 8% revenue target only if ROIC rises to 13.3%, implying margin expansion is already priced in. The model exposes the hidden claim: the company must either improve returns or reduce reinvestment intensity to make the growth story work. Without this discipline, 8% growth funded at 60% reinvestment but earning only 12% destroys value.

Where the approach breaks

Reinvestment discipline fails under two real scenarios: (1) Rapid sector disruption. Kodak's core photo business needed 80%+ reinvestment to stay competitive, but that high reinvestment earned <8% ROIC (below cost of capital), destroying value every year. The model was mathematically sound but captured a business in terminal decline. (2) Measurement ambiguity in capex. Tech companies often expense R&D (which is really growth capex) instead of capitalizing it, making reinvestment appear lower than reality. If Netflix's R&D is $3B annually but flows through P&L, the reinvestment calculation looks lean, but the business actually reinvests ~30% of operating cash flow. Ignoring this buried capex leads to over-optimistic free cash flow estimates and an inflated growth value.
