Terminal value

Overview Thesis Themes Subthemes Concepts Evidence Discussions Sessions

Concept

Terminal value

Meaning. Terminal value captures cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period under stable-growth assumptions.

Why it matters. It is often the largest part of a DCF, so small inconsistencies in mature growth, reinvestment, or risk can dominate the answer.

How Damodaran develops it. The course treats terminal value as a consistency test: stable growth must fit the economy, the firm, and the reinvestment economics.

Common mistakes

  • Using terminal growth higher than the economy forever.
  • Letting terminal value rescue an implausible story.

Worked example

A mature consumer staples firm has FCFF in year 5 = $120M, expected to grow at 2.5% forever. WACC = 8%. Terminal Value at end of year 5 = $120M × 1.025 / (0.08 - 0.025) = $123M / 0.055 = $2,236M. Discount this back 5 years at 8%: PV = $2,236M / (1.08^5) = $2,236M / 1.469 ≈ $1,522M. If the entire 10-year DCF value is $1,800M, terminal value is 85% of total—a $1M error in terminal growth assumptions swings the answer by $40M because of the infinite denominator.

Now test the boundary: if you had set terminal growth to 3% instead, TV becomes $123M / (0.08 - 0.03) = $2,460M, PV ≈ $1,674M. A 50 bps error in terminal growth adds $150M to firm value—43% more than the entire enterprise. This is why Damodaran insists terminal growth ≤ GDP growth and forces a reinvestment check: a 2.5% growth rate with FCFF of $120M requires reinvestment of $120M × (2.5% / 9%) ≈ $33.3M per year (using sales-to-capital ratio implied by sustainable growth).

Where the approach breaks

Terminal value dominates and breaks credibility when terminal growth creeps above long-run GDP growth. A tech company in 2020 might have been valued with terminal growth at 3.5% while GDP forecasts were 2%—impossible in steady state. Historical case: dot-com valuations in 1999 used terminal growth of 5-7% on companies that would have to be larger than the economy within 30 years. The analyst should see: if TV is >75% of total value and terminal growth is close to or above WACC, the model is not a valuation, it is a story carrying infinite uncertainty. Red flag: a 1% change in terminal growth swings value by 30-50%.
