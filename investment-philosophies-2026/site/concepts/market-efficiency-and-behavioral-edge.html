Concept Page

Market Efficiency and Behavioral Edge

Damodaran treats efficiency as uneven rather than absolute, asking where markets are fast, where they are slow, and where crowd behavior creates repeatable pricing errors.

Primary sessions: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27 behavioral financemarket structureconsumer attention

Core Idea

Efficiency is not a universal condition. Some information may be incorporated into price almost instantly, while other information moves through the market slowly, unevenly, or emotionally. Claimed edge depends not only on the information itself, but on who sees it, how they interpret it, and how crowded the reaction becomes.

Fast and efficient markets

In some settings, new information is absorbed so quickly that little edge remains for delayed or public reaction.

Slow learning

Different investors update at different speeds and with different confidence, which can leave room for drift and delayed adjustment.

Overreaction

Markets may process information emotionally, pushing price too far and creating later mean-reversion opportunities.

Pattern-based demand for control

Charts, momentum, and technical language remain attractive partly because they make noisy markets feel readable.

Why It Matters

Almost every active investing philosophy in the course depends on some version of this idea. Without a plausible claim about partial inefficiency, there is no durable story of edge.
