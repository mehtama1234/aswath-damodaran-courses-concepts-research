Concept Page

Philosophy Over Tactics

The course starts by separating visible investing moves from the deeper logic that explains why those moves should work, when they stop working, and who can practice them consistently.

Primary sessions: 1, 2, 6, 43 investor psychologydecision-makingeducation

Core Idea

A tactic tells you what to do. A philosophy tells you why it should work, what kind of market mistake it depends on, when it should stop working, and what kind of investor can actually live with it. Without that deeper layer, investors are vulnerable to every new screen, chart pattern, guru, or market story.

A philosophy filters noise

Investors without a philosophy are easier to distract because they have no stable model of what edge is supposed to look like.

A philosophy reveals hidden assumptions

Every style carries a view of human behavior underneath it, whether that is overreaction, slow learning, herding, or neglected information.

A philosophy allows adaptation

If a strategy weakens, the investor can ask whether the market changed, whether the tactic was misapplied, or whether the original claim was weak.

Valuation still anchors the debate

Different philosophies care about different sources of value, but most still need a way to connect expectations to price.

Why It Matters

This concept is the course's operating discipline. It keeps investors from confusing a tactic, screen, rumor, or guru recommendation with a durable view of edge.
