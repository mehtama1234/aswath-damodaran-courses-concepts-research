Information Trading and Social Processing

News does not move directly into price. It travels through authority, interpretation, rumor, investor attention, and reaction speed before it becomes market consensus.

Primary sessions: 23, 24, 25, 26, 27 information flowauthorityinterpretationreaction speed

Core Idea

Information does not enter the market as a pure fact and then mechanically become price. It moves through rumor, credibility, analyst filters, insider interpretation, investor attention, and demand-supply response. That makes information trading partly a question of social processing, not just data access.

Different people see different signals

A public filing or earnings report may be common information, but the market's interpretation of it can still vary widely.

Authority changes reaction

Insiders, analysts, and famous managers matter partly because the market treats them as legitimate interpreters.

Speed is uneven

If markets are fast, the edge disappears quickly. If markets are slow, there may be drift. If markets overreact, reversal may be the real opportunity.

Noise is inseparable from signal

Rumors, management changes, and corporate events create fertile ground for narrative excess and overinterpretation.

Why It Matters

This concept expands finance into social processing. It explains why public information can still generate uneven price reactions and why some voices move markets more than others.
