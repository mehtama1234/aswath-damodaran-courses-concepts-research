4 anchor sessions

Philosophy Over Tactics

The course argues that sustainable investing requires a coherent worldview about prices, behavior, and edge, not a pile of disconnected screens and tricks.

4 anchor sessions

Risk, Return, and Friction

Risk is treated as inseparable from opportunity, but realized returns are also shaped by costs, taxes, and the actual path an investor must survive.

6 anchor sessions

Market Mistakes and Behavioral Edge

A large part of the course asks when markets are efficient, when they are slow, and when human behavior creates repeatable pricing errors.

8 anchor sessions

Value Versus Growth Is Really About Where Value Lives

The course reframes the classic value-growth split as a disagreement about whether the best bargains live in existing assets or future growth assets.

7 anchor sessions

The Industrial Problem of Edge

Many philosophies sound attractive in isolation, but the course repeatedly asks whether they still work once many people know about them, compete for them, and commercialize them.

8 anchor sessions

The Allure of Control Versus the Case for Humility

Investors are drawn to timing, stock-picking, and active repositioning because these activities offer a feeling of agency, but the course keeps returning to how hard it is to earn that confidence.

7 anchor sessions

Investing Is Also Cultural, Social, and Institutional

Several parts of the course show that assets gain traction not only from cash flows, but from identity, trust, status, ideology, and the institutions investors either rely on or reject.

7 anchor sessions

Portfolio Fit Matters More Than Universal Recipes

The course ends by suggesting that the right philosophy depends on temperament, informational advantage, tax position, time horizon, and willingness to live with the consequences.
