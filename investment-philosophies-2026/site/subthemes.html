Investment Philosophies - The 2026 Edition - Subthemes

OverviewThesisThemesConceptsSubthemesDiscussionsSessions
Philosophy Over Tactics · sessions 1, 2
A strategy is not a philosophy

Buying low PE stocks, trading charts, or following insiders are not philosophies by themselves; they need a deeper explanation for why they should work.

Philosophy Over Tactics · sessions 2, 8, 12
Behavior sits underneath every investing style

Every philosophy makes an implicit claim about how investors think, misreact, herd, panic, or miss information.

Philosophy Over Tactics · sessions 6, 13, 18, 34
Valuation is the common language across styles

Even investors who disagree about what matters still need a way to connect expected cash flows, narratives, and prices.

Risk, Return, and Friction · sessions 3
Risk is danger plus opportunity

The course rejects the idea that risk is only a negative and insists that upside and downside are linked in investing.

Risk, Return, and Friction · sessions 3, 4, 5
Different assets hide different risks

Bond risk, equity risk, price risk, and cash-flow risk are not interchangeable, and investors often measure the wrong thing.

Risk, Return, and Friction · sessions 7, 36, 38
Implementation can destroy a good idea

High turnover, taxes, and trading friction mean that a theoretical edge may not survive real-world execution.

Market Mistakes and Behavioral Edge · sessions 8, 9, 23
Efficiency is not all-or-nothing

Markets can be fast in some places, slow in others, and different across asset classes, information types, and investor populations.

Market Mistakes and Behavioral Edge · sessions 10, 11, 12
Patterns attract believers because they promise control

Momentum, calendar effects, and charting appeal to investors partly because they turn noisy markets into readable stories.

Market Mistakes and Behavioral Edge · sessions 23, 24, 25, 26, 27
Information does not become price cleanly

News passes through rumors, processing delays, different interpretations, and incentive conflicts before it becomes market price.

Value Versus Growth Is Really About Where Value Lives · sessions 13, 14, 15, 16, 17
Value investing privileges assets in place

Value investors prefer businesses whose worth can be tied more directly to what already exists and can be analyzed with less speculation.

Value Versus Growth Is Really About Where Value Lives · sessions 18, 19, 20, 21, 22
Growth investing privileges underappreciated optionality

Growth investors are making a claim that the market is underestimating future expansion, reinvestment quality, or strategic upside.

Value Versus Growth Is Really About Where Value Lives · sessions 16, 21
Activism appears on both sides

Both value and growth can become interventionist when investors stop waiting for recognition and start trying to change outcomes.

The Industrial Problem of Edge · sessions 9, 14, 25, 37
Public strategies decay when they become crowded

Simple screens, public signals, and copied rules lose power as they become easier to identify, package, and arbitrage away.

The Industrial Problem of Edge · sessions 28, 29
True arbitrage is scarce and temporary

Pure arbitrage is rare precisely because obvious riskless profits attract capital and disappear quickly.

The Industrial Problem of Edge · sessions 30, 42
Many branded edges are really speculative bets

The course warns that the language of arbitrage or skill is often used to market trades that still carry meaningful risk.

The Allure of Control Versus the Case for Humility · sessions 31, 32, 33, 34, 35
Market timing is the ultimate control fantasy

Timing promises that investors can improve outcomes not just by choosing assets well, but by getting in and out at the right moments.

The Allure of Control Versus the Case for Humility · sessions 33, 34, 35
Being right is not enough; you must be right on schedule

Timing strategies fail because they require correct judgment, correct sequencing, and behavioral stamina at the same time.

The Allure of Control Versus the Case for Humility · sessions 36, 37, 38
Passive investing is a philosophy of disciplined humility

The passive case is not laziness; it is a sober response to the empirical weakness and inconsistency of active outperformance.

Investing Is Also Cultural, Social, and Institutional · sessions 24, 25, 36
Authority and influence shape information markets

Insiders, analysts, and portfolio managers matter not just because they may know more, but because the market assigns them social authority.

Investing Is Also Cultural, Social, and Institutional · sessions 39, 40, 42
Alternative assets often sell identity as much as return

Collectibles, private assets, and certain alternatives gain appeal from exclusivity, taste, signaling, and stories of uniqueness.

Investing Is Also Cultural, Social, and Institutional · sessions 41
Bitcoin emerges from distrust in institutions

Crypto is framed as an asset class born from skepticism toward governments, banks, and centralized verification systems.

Portfolio Fit Matters More Than Universal Recipes · sessions 2, 31, 43
A philosophy must match the investor, not just the spreadsheet

A strategy can be analytically sound and still fail if the investor cannot hold it through drawdowns, complexity, or social pressure.

Portfolio Fit Matters More Than Universal Recipes · sessions 7, 38, 39, 42
Asset choice is inseparable from implementation constraints

Taxes, liquidity, time horizon, and operational simplicity strongly influence what should actually go into a portfolio.

Portfolio Fit Matters More Than Universal Recipes · sessions 36, 38, 43
The end goal is consistency, not style collecting

The final synthesis is not to know every philosophy, but to choose one or a few that can be practiced coherently over time.
