# Project Objective

## End-to-End Goal

Build a research-grade Damodaran course intelligence workspace that turns raw
playlist transcripts into structured, readable, and reusable knowledge
products. The work should not stop at transcript capture or thin summaries. It
should produce a durable library of themes, subthemes, concepts, evidence,
cross-course comparisons, and plain-language synthesis that can support future
course-building, sector analysis, company analysis, investment thinking, and
capital-allocation research.

## What "Done" Actually Means

The workspace should eventually make all of the following true.

1. Every target course has a complete, auditable transcript corpus with correct
   lecture ordering, metadata, clean transcript text, and cue-level evidence.
2. Each course has a serious analytical layer covering:
   - major themes
   - detailed subthemes
   - recurring concepts
   - tensions, tradeoffs, and decision rules
   - evidence maps back to concrete sessions and transcript passages
3. The root workspace explains how the courses connect to one another rather
   than treating them as isolated folders.
4. The root workspace produces rich but easy-to-understand writeups of the
   broader societal, cultural, consumer, industrial, managerial, and market
   patterns visible across the material.
5. The output is publishable and reusable through HTML pages and structured
   JSON artifacts, so it can support later downstream work without needing the
   original transcript-mining effort to be repeated.

## Core Workstreams

### 1. Course Ingestion

For each Damodaran course in scope:

- collect the full playlist manifest
- download or extract every available transcript
- normalize titles, sequence, and metadata
- preserve clean text and cue-level transcript evidence
- maintain a summary or inventory layer so completeness can be audited quickly

### 2. Theme And Subtheme Extraction

For each course:

- identify the major teaching themes
- break each major theme into detailed subthemes
- capture repeated arguments, recurring mental models, and characteristic
  distinctions
- keep the structure explicit enough that it can support future course design
  and analytical reuse

### 3. Concept Library Construction

Across and within courses:

- define reusable concepts
- normalize overlapping ideas that appear in different courses or for different
  audiences
- preserve course-specific variants when the framing or teaching purpose
  changes
- maintain evidence links so concept pages are traceable back to source
  sessions

### 4. Big-Picture Synthesis

Write plain-English, detailed synthesis explaining the deeper patterns across
the active workspace, including:

- societal patterns: trust, institutions, legitimacy, collective behavior,
  ideology, and the social framing of markets
- cultural patterns: narrative, prestige, style, fashion, mood, investor
  temperament, and how stories gain authority
- consumer patterns: demand stories, brand, loyalty, premium positioning,
  adoption, and the gap between excitement and economic proof
- industrial patterns: different business systems, asset types, ownership
  structures, life-cycle conditions, and how analytical treatment changes by
  sector and operating reality
- managerial and capital-allocation patterns: incentives, governance,
  reinvestment, financing choices, payout logic, and the discipline of acting
  under constraints
- pedagogical patterns: how Damodaran changes language, pacing, scaffolding,
  and emphasis depending on the audience

## Required Outputs

The workspace should keep building toward these concrete outputs:

- course transcript inventories
- course session pages
- course theme and subtheme maps
- course concept libraries
- evidence maps
- root normalized concept atlas
- cross-course comparison pages
- audience-comparison pages where relevant
- long-form root synthesis essays
- reusable JSON registries backing the HTML outputs

## Why This Matters

The real value of the project is not archival completeness by itself. The goal
is to build a reasoning library that makes Damodaran's teaching operational for
other uses.

That includes:

- designing new courses from the extracted structure
- building sector and industry writeups from the concept scaffolding
- writing company-level analyses that connect business story, market narrative,
  valuation logic, and economic discipline
- comparing how the same idea changes across audiences, contexts, and business
  types

## Standard For Future Additions

New course or root-level work should move the workspace toward more depth, more
clarity, and more reuse. Thin summaries are not enough. The target standard is
rich, transcript-backed interpretation that remains readable to a non-specialist
while still being analytically serious.
