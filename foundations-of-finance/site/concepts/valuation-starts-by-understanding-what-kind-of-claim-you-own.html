Valuation Starts By Understanding What Kind Of Claim You Own

The course builds valuation from the ground up by distinguishing contractual, residual, and contingent claims and then showing how each maps into a different pricing structure.

Different Claims, Different Logic

The course does not treat valuation as one universal formula. Bonds, equity, and options differ because the structure of the claim changes the structure of the payoff.

Why Claim Structure Comes First

A bond holder is promised contractual cash flows. An equity owner gets what is left over. An option holder benefits only in specific states of the world. If the analyst misses that hierarchy, every later valuation step becomes confused.

What This Enables Later

Once claim structure is clear, more advanced Damodaran courses can build naturally into intrinsic valuation, pricing, private-company issues, optionality, and control. This theme is the compact origin point for that later complexity.

Subtheme

Equity is a residual claim on what is left after everyone else is paid

Equity valuation begins by recognizing that stockholders do not own promised cash flows; they own what survives after debt payments, reinvestment, and every other prior claim.

Subtheme

Options reveal how contingency changes value logic

The option-valuation introduction widens the course beyond fixed and residual claims, showing that some assets are valuable because they pay off only when particular states of the world occur.
